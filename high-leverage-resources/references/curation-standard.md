# Curation Standard

Use this reference after the topic is clear. It governs research, selection, output, and repair without enlarging `SKILL.md`.

## 1. Candidate research and evidence

Research broadly enough to compare credible candidates, then retain only five. For every selected resource, record:

- exact title and creator, organization, or publisher;
- type and intended learner;
- a **primary-source URL** (publisher, instructor, official course page, official docs, or organizer);
- access status and cost **as checked today**; say `price varies` rather than guessing;
- an independent quality/currency signal only when it changes the decision (for example, a respected syllabus, an active official release history, or a credible review).

Primary sources establish availability; they do not alone prove quality. Avoid ranking by raw review count, social-media popularity, SEO pages, affiliate lists, or unverifiable claims such as “the best course.” For new or niche subjects, say when the evidence is thin and explain the least-risky choice.

## 2. Selection rubric

Assess candidates against the learner's outcome, not a universal leaderboard:

| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| Outcome fit | Does it teach the requested capability at the stated level? |
| Signal density | Does it reach useful concepts or practice without avoidable filler? |
| Practice value | Can the learner test, build, solve, or apply something? |
| Authority and currency | Is its source credible and current enough for this topic? |
| Accessibility | Does its language, price, format, and time demand fit the learner? |

Choose a complementary set: normally one foundation, one guided explanation, one hands-on or reference resource, one depth resource, and one continuing-practice or community source. This is a preference, not a quota. Do not include a weak category merely to look diverse.

```markdown

## Curator's note
[Scope, learner assumption, and the selection trade-off in 2–3 sentences.]

## The five resources

### 1. [Exact title]
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Type | [type] |
| Level | [beginner/intermediate/advanced] |
| Access | [free/paid/price varies, checked date] |
| Best for | [specific learner need] |
| Verify | [primary-source link] |

**Why it made the cut**  
[Outcome-specific rationale, with evidence kept distinct from judgment.]

**Use it this week**  
[Named, verifiable chapter/module/page when available; otherwise a concrete task.] 

**Deprioritize for this goal**  
[Source-backed limitation, or a transparent goal-based choice. Never label unverified material as low quality.]

[Repeat this exact structure for resources 2–5.]

## Recommended order
| Step | Resource | Why now |
|---|---|---|

## Seven-day plan
| Day | Specific task and resource | Time | Concrete outcome |
|---|---|---|---|

## After week one
[One practice step and one direction for deeper study.]
```

The resource section headings may be translated to match the user's language, but all fields remain. Use tables only for fields and the plan; keep reasoning readable as prose.

## 4. Seven-day plan rules

- Use only the five selected resources; no hidden sixth “quick video” or generic web search.
- Fit the user's stated daily time. With no constraint, target 1–2 hours, but do not pad short tasks.
- Assign an observable outcome each day: a worked exercise, a small artifact, an explanation, a decision, or a review note.
- Name a lesson, chapter, or module only after confirming its title. If detail is unavailable, write an honest task such as “complete the official quickstart through its first working example.”
- Put foundational work before stretch material; include at least one practice/retrieval day and one review/next-step day.

## 5. Final self-repair checklist

Before delivery, verify:

1. The topic, learner assumption, and scope are visible.
2. There are exactly five distinct, currently available named resources.
3. Every resource has a primary-source URL and checked access note.
4. Claimed prices, structure, dates, reviews, and “skip” advice are sourced or explicitly framed as a goal-based recommendation.
5. The five choices are complementary or any same-format exception is explained.
6. Every plan day names selected resources only, fits the budget, and produces a concrete outcome.
7. The language, headings, links, and tables are easy to scan.
8. The output file path is reported only after a successful write.
